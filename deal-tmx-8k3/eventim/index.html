Executive summary

Your hypothesis is half right — and the true half is better ammunition

What the record confirms

  • Yoni exited Zappa completely in Sept 2019 — 17 months before the Eventim ticketing JV, 4 years before Eventim took over Zappa itself.
  • His creation, Zappa Tickets — the group's most profitable arm — became the base of Eventim Israel's market leadership. Under someone else's ownership.
  • So yes: he factually missed the entire Eventim chapter. The platform seat went to Golan Einat and later Delek Israel.

What the record refutes

  • He did not lose money. He sold at ₪80M (2019). Zappa's valuation four years later, post-Eventim: ₪85–90M — with heavy debt and lawsuits. Arguably he sold at the top.
  • No ticketing company from inside Zappa ever IPO'd or hit $100M. The only public $100M+ company in the story is CTS Eventim itself (~€5.8B).
  • The sale price "never made public"? It was in four newspapers simultaneously.
The real insight: Yoni watched both movies. He watched his ticketing baby become a giant's beachhead without him — and he watched the man who stayed get diluted from 100% to 19%, inject ₪30M more, and end up suing for transparency. One scar says "never miss the seat again." The other says "never be the minority partner of a giant." Both are sitting at our table.

The lesson Yoni internalized

In every market, the upside — data, e-commerce, cross-border inventory, market-cap growth — accrued to the party holding the platform and the majority. The local partner contributed access and inventory, then got diluted, bought down, or bought out.

toMix's 30%+ demand on Flowz is this exact playbook — learned from the receiving end, now being run from the giving end. He is building his own Eventim Israel. In his script, we are Zappa Tickets.

The psychology

Two scars, one negotiator

Scar 1 · The seat he missed

He built Israel's best ticketing asset, sold everything in Sept 2019, and 17 months later watched it become the foundation of the market leader — with a Frankfurt-listed giant on top and his old partner collecting the strategic position.

What it produces now: "This time I hold the platform. This time I get the big stake. This time the tech company integrates into my rails."

Scar 2 · The partner who got crushed

The man who "won" the Eventim relationship — Einat — went from 100% owner to 19% minority, injected ₪30M more, watched his artists walk, and had to litigate to see the ticketing books.

What it produces now: deep distrust of structures where he's the minority of someone else's vehicle — including SAFEs, VC logic, and "trust the cap table" arguments. "We're not a hi-tech company."

Both scars are real and rational — the record backs them. Which means neither can be argued away. They can only be structurally answered: give him a real seat where the upside genuinely lives, and never ask him to be the powerless minority of our vehicle. That is exactly what One House + warrants does — if we frame it through his history.

Israel-only entertainment assets in this story cap out at ₪80–100M valuations — Zappa never escaped that band in 20 years. The upside worth owning is global or it is nothing. Which is precisely the argument for his stake sitting in Flowz Global — the only room where his 30%-instinct can ever be worth what he wants it to be worth.

Using it in the room

Lines that heal the scar — and lines that rip it open

Say

The seat
"You've seen what happens when the tech layer and the rails are owned separately — someone always ends up carved out. We're offering the opposite: your stake sits where the whole story lives, not in one territory of it."
The upside
"An Israel-only entertainment company tops out around ₪100M — your market has proven that for twenty years. Equity in the global company is the only version of this where your percentage can mean real money."
The trust problem
"Every shekel of value you give — data, champions, distribution — gets priced and converted into ownership. Not promises: a formula, with audit rights on both sides. Full transparency, contractually — no one should ever need a lawsuit to see the numbers."
The rails
"Your ticketing venture wins because our layer makes it unbeatable in Israel — exclusively. That's worth more to toMix than any minority stake in us: it's the position, not the paper."

Never say

The wound
Anything that names Zappa, Eventim, or "the deal you missed." He was never in that deal — implying he lost it is both wrong and humiliating. The history informs our framing; it must never become an argument.
The trap
"Trust us, it's standard" / "that's how startups do it." Standard startup paper is exactly what his world has watched fail (Eventer, ₪6M). Every mechanism needs a cash-logic explanation, not a hi-tech-convention one.
The diminishment
"You'd only get 3–4%." Never lead with the small number. Lead with what the percentage is of, the formula that grows it, and the Israel package that comes with it. The number lands last.
