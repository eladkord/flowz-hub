Where we are

Three dimensions, no rules of engagement

Today lifecycle, tiers and tags live side by side with no defined relationship. Each one is fine alone – together they collide.

Lifecycle

New · Rising · Core · At-risk · Lapsed. A temporal state – but "VIP" used to sit inside it, mixing status into a time axis. Recency runs on calendar days, which breaks for annual events.

Tiers

Bronze · Silver · Gold · Platinum. Understandable but generic – and nothing defines what earns a tier: events? money? both? "Veteran" wants in but measures a different thing (tenure).

Tags

EarlyBuyer, GroupBuyer, Spender, Responsive... applied ad-hoc, no thresholds, no manager view, and no line between what a tag is versus what a tier is.

The research verdict: the three-field decomposition is industry-correct. What is missing is the connective tissue – one engine underneath, clear boundaries between the axes, and names a stranger ranks in under 2 seconds.
The proposal

Three fields, one engine

Every attendee action lands in an append-only ledger. Three engines read it, three fields surface it. Nothing can contradict, and every threshold recomputes cleanly from history when a client edits it later.

The Ledger

Append-only record of value events per attendee:

🎟️ ticket purchased · edition #
🏨 room / F&B / merch spend
📩 open · click · reply · WA / support
👥 group size · buy timing · referral
→
Lifecycle Temporal state: New → Returning → Regular → Cooling → Lapsed. Recency counted in missed editions, never calendar days. Engagement splits Cooling into "reachable" vs "silent". Recency + Engagement
Tier Earned status: events attended and LTV over a rolling window – one VIP table can't buy the top tier. Upgrades instantly, downgrades only at season end with a grace window. Frequency + Monetary
Badges Behavior patterns, auto-computed from editable rules: Early Bird, Group Buyer, Big Spender, Veteran, Advocate. Plus a separate freeform manual layer (Artist, Helper). Pattern rules
The boundary rule: rankable → Tier · temporal state → Lifecycle · behavior pattern → Badge
The logic

Where each signal lives – the five questions

SignalLives inWhy
1 · Events attendedTier inputRankable. Counted over a rolling 24 months in 3–4 bins – quintiles over "attended 1, 2 or 3 events" is fake precision.
2 · Rooms / F&B revenueTier inputMonetary sub-score. Tier needs events AND LTV, so the rooms-always buyer with 2 events can outrank a GA-only buyer with 4.
3 · Group buyersBadgeNot rankable. Their money already counts in Monetary; the badge captures the pattern for group offers and referral flows.
4 · Early / late buyersBadgeGold for pricing flows, meaningless as rank. Early Bird = first-window buyer 2+ times; Last-Minute = final-week buyer.
5 · Engagement scoreLifecycle modifierRatio with decay (share of messages engaged, events attended vs opportunity). Splits identical recency states: "Cooling · reachable" vs "Cooling · silent" – different flow each. Never feeds the tier: blended scores hide the driver.
Why engagement stays out of the tier: a fused "health score" is the documented failure mode – green account, renews at 78%. Multi-dimension views predict churn ~34% better. Spend and engagement stay visibly separate.
Tier mechanics

Status you earn, not a label that flickers

↑ Upgrade instantly

The moment the threshold is crossed, the attendee levels up. Goal-gradient works for us: always show progress-to-next-tier on the profile.

↓ Downgrade gently

Only at season end, with notice and a grace window – "attend 1 event to keep Backstage". Demoted customers measurably end up less loyal than never-promoted ones, so demotion is engineered, not automatic. Tier freezes during the producer's off-season.

📅 Event-relative recency

"Lapsed" means missed 2 consecutive editions they used to attend – never "no purchase in 365 days", which marks every annual-festival fan dead in month 11. Cadence detected per producer from their event calendar.

🔒 Sustained qualification

Tier = events AND LTV over the trailing window. Qualifying metric stays isolated from anything spendable (future points program), so a redemption can never demote anyone – the Salesforce currency split, adopted early.

ApproachWhat it isVerdict
