The concept

Anatomy of a Decision Deck

01

Context from live data

Slides generated from the system's own numbers: the insight, the trend, what others do, what it costs. Not pasted screenshots – queries.

Example: "Repeat-buyer rate down 9% vs last event. 214 lapsed VIPs. Here's the revenue at stake."
02

Framed choices

Closed options with a recommended default and a "decision lens" explaining the tradeoff. Multi-select where it fits. No blank canvases.

Example: "Win-back approach: personal call · automated sequence · bundled offer (recommended)."
03

Commitment surface

Answered async at the desk or live at the round table. Owner per decision, quorum if needed, notes for the choices the chips don't capture.

Delivery: calendar invite + Organization Hub link + password. Offline copy for the flight.
04

DONE = execution

Marking done is not closing a file. Choices become tasks, flows get scheduled, owners get assigned, and the decision enters the permanent log with its full context.

Six months later: "Why did we drop the early-bird tier?" – open the log, the whole deck is the answer.

Market scan

Everyone holds one piece. Nobody closes the loop.

🗣️ Meeting & decision tools

  • AI notetakers (Fireflies 20M users, Fellow, Circleback): every summary now ships a "Decisions" section – proof leaders want decisions isolated. All passive: they report what was said, never frame what to decide.
  • Loomio: real closed-choice voting + published outcomes – and zero execution. A co-op niche for 14 years.
  • Cloverpop: a decade + $12.6M selling a "system of record for decisions" to enterprises. Survived only by fleeing upmarket to P&G/Sanofi – a horizontal decision tool arrives empty.
  • Execution stops at task stubs everywhere. Compiling a decision into running processes: untouched.

🖼️ Interactive deck platforms

  • Menti / Slido: capture the room's opinion, end as an export file.
  • Pitch / Gamma: watch-the-reader analytics; a viewer's choice triggers nothing. Tome died at <$4M ARR on 20M users – generic AI decks don't monetize.
  • Storydoc: in-deck input syncing to a CRM sells – but for outbound sales, not governance.
  • Zeck (10k execs): async board pre-votes → permanent minutes. Closest cousin – and it terminates in legal records, not tasks. Exec-authored, never data-generated.
  • "Recommended default" appears in zero deck tools. The name "Decision Deck" is unclaimed in SaaS.

🧠 Decision intelligence

  • A formal Gartner category: rated "transformational" 2025, first Magic Quadrant Jan 2026, market sized $13–18B growing 16–30% CAGR.
  • But the leaders (FICO, SAS, IBM, Quantexa, Aera) automate machine-speed decisions – credit, fraud, supply chain. The human/operator side is nearly unoccupied.
  • Decision registries are demanded and die: ~50% of ADR repos abandon under 5 records; Almanac shut down 2025; Loqbooq 2024. Failure mode: logging is a separate act after the decision.
  • SMB proof: EOS runs a weekly decision ritual in 275k companies; Ninety digitizes it for 18k – hand-typed, data-blind, execution-dead.
The whitespace verdict: the market splits into capture-opinion, watch-the-reader, auto-refresh-the-report and record-the-meeting. Nobody runs data → framed choices with a default → commitment → compiled execution → permanent log in one artifact. Best-in-class covers 2 of those 5 steps. The realistic fast-follows (Zeck moving down from boards, Decisions adding data-generated agendas) are each one architectural leap away – and neither owns the operator's live business data the way Flowz does.
The need

Why organizations will pay for this

The quantified pain

37%of meetings produce an identifiable decision or committed action. Two thirds end with nothing executable. (Flowtrace)
71%of senior managers call meetings unproductive; a 30-minute meeting costs $700–1,600 – Shopify put the calculator in the invite and cut 12,000 recurring meetings. (HBR · Shopify)
58%+of managers' deciding time is used ineffectively – roughly $250M/yr of wasted wages at a typical Fortune 500. (McKinsey)
95%correlation between decision effectiveness and top-tier financial performance, across 1,000+ companies over 10 years. (Bain)
2×faster decisions with half the meetings when the choice is framed and structured. (Cloverpop research, 566 decisions)

Three behaviors already proven

SMBs sustain decision rituals. EOS's weekly Level-10 meeting runs in 275,000 companies; Ninety.io digitizes it for 18,000. Operators will pay for structured deciding – today it's hand-typed and data-blind.
The canon prescribes our exact format. Amazon's two-way doors and "decide at 70% of the information", GitLab's DRI, Bain's RAPID: frame the choice, name the decider, bias to a default, keep reversible calls lightweight.
The graveyard names the fix. Decision logs keep failing (ADRs abandoned, Almanac dead, Loqbooq dead) because logging is a separate act after the decision. In a Decision Deck the log is a byproduct of pressing DONE – the exact failure mode, inverted.
Positioning rule from the research: never sell "decision intelligence" to producers – that term now means enterprise fraud engines. Sell fewer meetings, decisions that execute themselves, and a permanent answer to "why did we do it this way last season". Seasonal, high-turnover event teams feel org-memory pain hardest.
Product shape

Three horizons, with gates between them

H1 · Core feature

Build now

Decision Decks inside Flowz, born from insights and Process flows.

  • "Open a Decision Deck" on every insight card
  • Deck builder: context slides from data + closed choices + defaults
  • Calendar invite + hub publish (login, offline copy)
  • DONE → tasks + scheduled flows + decision log entry
Gate to H2: producers open decks we didn't ask them to open, and the log gets consulted ("why did we…") more than once a month.

H2 · Module

On pull

The Decision Hub: a first-class module alongside CRM and Tasks.

  • Decision log as a browsable, searchable registry
  • Templates: pricing call, lineup call, budget approval
  • Recurring decision cadences (weekly revenue round table)
  • Outcome tracking: "decided X, result was Y"
Gate to H3: clients route non-event decisions through it (hiring, suppliers) – the tool outgrows its data.

H3 · The fork

If earned

A standalone product built around the loop – the decision OS for operators.

  • Data-agnostic: connect any source, AI frames the deck
  • Org Hub as the system of record for "why"
  • Sold to SMB operators, not enterprise analysts
  • Working name: Roundtable by Flowz
Discipline: the fork happens only on demonstrated pull. Features that fork early die as thin clones of both parents.
Strategy

Why this could be core, and what would kill it

The strategic logic

The wedgeEvent producers make weekly, high-stakes, recoverable decisions (pricing, lineup, spend) in small teams that are always on the move. Perfect first user.
The moatEvery decided deck trains the next one: defaults get smarter, templates accumulate, and the decision log becomes switching-cost gravity no dashboard has.
Why nowAI is what makes deck-framing free. What took us a research sweep and a build session becomes a button: data → framed options → recommended default.
The reframeFlowz stops being "a CRM for events" and becomes "the system that runs the organization's decisions" – with events as the beachhead vertical.

Honest risks

🪤
Feature-not-product trapThe standalone graveyard is real: Cloverpop fled upmarket after a decade, Almanac and Loqbooq shut down, Tome died at <$4M ARR. H1 is a feature; the fork must be earned.
🎓
Fast-follow exposureZeck moving down from boardrooms, or Decisions adding data-generated agendas – each is one leap away. Our defense is owning the operator's live data and the execution tail.
🪑
Meeting culture inertiaSome orgs simply won't decide in an artifact. The deck must also work as the agenda ON the table, not only instead of it.
📵
Garbage framing riskAn AI deck with wrong options is worse than no deck. Human review stays in the loop until defaults earn trust, decision by decision.
